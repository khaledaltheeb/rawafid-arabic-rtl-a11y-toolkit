# Code of Conduct

The Rawafid Arabic/RTL Accessibility & Localization Toolkit is an open technical project. Participation should remain safe, respectful, evidence-oriented, and accessible across languages, cultures, disabilities, experience levels, and communication styles.

## Our commitment

We are committed to a community in which people can contribute in good faith without harassment, discrimination, intimidation, retaliation, or exclusionary conduct. Technical disagreement is expected; personal hostility is not.

Because this project works across Arabic/RTL, accessibility, localization, Unicode, security, and open-source engineering, contributors should take particular care with differences in language fluency, cultural context, disability, assistive-technology use, and technical background.

## Expected behavior

Participants are expected to:

- engage respectfully and honestly;
- critique code, evidence, standards interpretations, and decisions rather than people;
- give and receive constructive feedback without humiliation or hostility;
- respect stated boundaries and requests to stop unwanted conduct;
- acknowledge mistakes and repair harm where practical;
- protect confidential, personal, security-sensitive, or embargoed information;
- credit sources and preserve contributor attribution;
- avoid overstating accessibility, security, standards, research, institutional, or adoption claims;
- disclose relevant conflicts of interest when advocating for decisions that may materially benefit them or an affiliated organization;
- use project channels for their intended purpose rather than for persistent unrelated promotion or pressure.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not acceptable in project spaces or while officially representing the project:

- harassment, threats, stalking, intimidation, or repeated unwanted attention;
- discriminatory, degrading, or dehumanizing comments or imagery;
- sexualized conduct or unwelcome sexual attention;
- deliberate misgendering or other targeted identity-based hostility;
- publishing another person's private information without permission;
- retaliation against someone for raising a concern or participating in a review;
- impersonation or misleading identity used to evade moderation or accountability;
- knowingly submitting plagiarized, unlicensed, fabricated, or materially misleading evidence;
- attempts to manipulate contribution, review, security, or governance processes through harassment, brigading, or coercion;
- persistent disruption after a moderator or maintainer has asked that the conduct stop.

A disagreement, rejected pull request, standards dispute, or critical technical review is not by itself a Code of Conduct violation. The relevant question is how participants behave while resolving it.

## Scope

This Code applies to:

- repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, and other project-managed online spaces;
- project email and other official communication channels;
- events, meetings, or working sessions explicitly organized on behalf of the project;
- public activity performed while officially representing Rawafid or this toolkit.

Serious conduct outside project spaces may be considered when it directly creates a credible safety or participation risk inside the project.

## Reporting a concern

Report Code of Conduct concerns privately to **contact@healthrenewal.org** with the subject `Rawafid Toolkit conduct report`.

Include the smallest amount of information needed to understand the incident: relevant dates, project spaces, links or screenshots where appropriate, people involved, and any immediate safety concern. Do not send passwords, credentials, private medical information, or unrelated personal data.

Do not use a public GitHub issue for a report that would expose private or sensitive information. Security vulnerabilities belong in the private process described in [SECURITY.md](./SECURITY.md).

## Review, confidentiality, and conflicts

Reports should be handled by a maintainer or designated reviewer who is not directly involved in the reported incident. Information will be shared only as necessary to assess the report, protect participants, and carry out a response.

The project is currently founder-led, so independent internal review may not always be available. If a report concerns the project steward, or every available maintainer has a material conflict, the implicated person must not make the final enforcement decision about their own conduct. The project will seek a neutral external reviewer or community advisor where practical and will document the existence of that conflict without publishing confidential report details.

No participant will be penalized merely for submitting a good-faith report that is ultimately found not to constitute a violation. Deliberately false or bad-faith reports intended to harass others may themselves violate this Code.

## Proportionate response

Responses should consider severity, impact, context, repetition, willingness to stop, and risk to community safety. Available actions include:

1. **Clarification or correction** — a private request to change wording, behavior, or process when harm appears limited and repair is straightforward.
2. **Formal warning** — a documented warning identifying the unacceptable conduct and the behavior required going forward.
3. **Temporary restriction** — temporary limits on participation, review, contact, or project spaces where a cooling-off or safety boundary is needed.
4. **Removal or permanent exclusion** — removal of content or permanent exclusion from project spaces for severe, repeated, retaliatory, or dangerous conduct.

The project may skip levels when the seriousness or immediacy of the risk requires it. Enforcement should be based on behavior and impact, not status, contribution volume, affiliation, or technical seniority.

## Appeals and governance learning

Where a meaningful enforcement action is taken, the affected participant may request reconsideration by an uninvolved maintainer or, when available, an external neutral reviewer. Reconsideration does not require disclosure of a reporter's identity or confidential evidence beyond what is necessary for procedural fairness.

Recurring conduct or moderation problems should feed back into project governance. The goal is not to build bureaucracy, but to make implicit expectations explicit and improve the structures that failed to prevent or resolve the problem.

## Changes to this Code

Material changes should be proposed publicly through a pull request or documented governance discussion so contributors can understand the reason for the change. Administrative corrections such as contact details, broken links, or clearer security-routing instructions may be updated directly when they do not alter the policy's intent.
